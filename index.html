01 · Executive Summary

The strongest month in the portfolio's history

June closed at £79,201 in rental revenue across the portfolio, the highest monthly figure on record and +14.8% versus May. On the comparable set of 27 listings (excluding the seven units onboarded during June), revenue reached £75,784, with occupancy at 81.1% against a market average of 53.7%. The portfolio sold 657 nights in June versus 574 in May while holding average rate essentially flat, which is exactly the outcome the pricing strategy was built to deliver: fill first, then compress rate upward as demand proves itself.

03 · Where the Growth Came From

Three engines pulled in the same direction

June's gain over May breaks down cleanly into three components. The stable core grew organically, four units that sat dark in May were brought back online and monetised immediately, and the seven newly onboarded listings contributed from their first days live.

May to June revenue bridge (£)
Comparable core plus reactivations plus new onboarding equals the record month

The reactivation line deserves attention: M16 0ZN, M4 2AD (110), M5 4BZ (40) and M3 6JN (310) produced zero revenue in May and delivered a combined £6,453 in June. M16 0ZN alone posted £2,380 at a £125 ADR and a Market Penetration Index of 198, the highest in the portfolio, in its first month back. Getting dormant inventory selling at healthy rates, not just selling, is where the strategy compounded.

04 · Market Position

Why ADR Index sits below 100, and why that is the plan

In June, 25 of 27 comparable listings priced below the market's average rate. Read in isolation that looks like underperformance. Read together with occupancy it tells the opposite story: 23 of those 25 listings beat the market on occupancy, most by 50 to 90 points, and 24 of 27 listings beat the market on RevPAR, the metric that actually measures money earned per available night. The median RevPAR Index was 115, meaning the typical Serenity listing out-earned its comp set by 15% despite charging less per night.

The Manchester market ran at roughly 53% occupancy in June. In a market where the average competitor's unit sits empty every other night, holding out for the market's average rate means inheriting the market's vacancy. The strategy instead prices to capture the demand that exists, which is why the portfolio ran at 81% while the comp set ran at 54%. The two listings that achieved rate parity, M11 4DB (ADR Index 106, RevPAR Index 192) and M5 3NW (ADR Index 103, RevPAR Index 182), show the ceiling: as booking pace on high-occupancy units keeps proving out, rates are being walked up unit by unit, which is already visible in the same-store ADR climbing 6.4% since March.

Reading the three indices together

MPI (occupancy vs. market): portfolio at 151, meaning Serenity filled at 1.5x the market's pace.

ADR Index (rate vs. market): deliberately below 100 while the market is soft; being pushed upward month over month.

RevPAR Index (revenue per available night): the verdict metric. Median 115, with 24 of 27 listings above market. When RevPAR Index beats 100, the occupancy-for-rate trade is generating more cash than the market's approach, full stop.

08 · July Focus

Converting a record June into a stronger July

PRIORITY 01

Walk rates up on saturated units

Eleven listings ran at or above 90% occupancy in June. These have demonstrably more demand than supply; base and weekend rates are being raised in steps so occupancy is converted into rate gains, following the M11 4DB and M5 3NW template.

PRIORITY 02

Ramp the June cohort

The M3 6GJ and M3 6JN units need review velocity and calendar visibility. Introductory positioning stays aggressive for the first bookings, then steps up automatically as each unit collects reviews, mirroring M4 4GP (417)'s ramp.

PRIORITY 03

Resolve M5 4SU NEW and defend July pace

M5 4SU NEW gets a pricing and restriction reset this week. Portfolio-wide, July already holds £69.3k on the books; pickup over the next three weeks is being managed to close the £10k gap to June's record.
